# Use Case: Finding a Clean Chinese Restaurant for Dinner with Friends

### Who is the User?

* **Persona/Role:** Emily Chen, a first-year undergraduate student at UW.
* **Context:** Emily recently moved to Seattle and is not familiar with Chinese restaurants near UW. She lives in a student dormitory and wants to have dinner with three friends after class. She has a limited budget and prefers restaurants that are clean, affordable, and close to campus.

### What Do They Want to Do with the Tool?

* **Primary Tasks:**
  * Find Chinese restaurants within a 15-minute walk from UW.
  * Check restaurant food safety inspection records.
  * Find restaurants with meals under $20 per person.
  * View restaurant menus and locations.
* **Operational Workflows:**
  * **Standard Operations:** Emily opens the Chinese Restaurant Guide and searches for restaurants near UW. She compares walking distances, checks food safety inspection records, and opens menus to compare prices. She selects a restaurant within a 15-minute walk that has meals under $20 and no recent serious food safety violations.
  * **Edge/Exception Operations:** If no restaurants meet all her requirements, Emily increases the walking distance to 20 minutes or raises her budget to $25. If a restaurant has no recent inspection records or menu information, she checks other restaurants with more complete information.

### What Needs and Desires Do They Have for the Tool?

* **Functional Needs:** The tool must provide restaurant names, locations, walking distances, food safety inspection records, and menu links. It should allow Emily to search for nearby restaurants and compare their information.
* **Desires / Experience Preferences:** Emily wants a simple and clear interface that is easy to use on her phone. She prefers restaurants to be displayed on a map with walking distances and clear food safety information. She wants to find a restaurant quickly without opening many different websites.

### What is Their Skill Level?

* **Technical Proficiency:** Medium. Emily regularly uses smartphones, Google Maps, and websites but has never used a restaurant food safety search tool.
* **Domain Knowledge:** Emily knows how to search for restaurants and compare menu prices, but she is not familiar with local Chinese restaurants or how to understand official food safety inspection records.

# Use Case: Finding a Quick and Affordable Chinese Dinner Between Classes

### Who is the User?

* **Persona/Role:** David Wang, a master's student in Mechanical Engineering at UW.
* **Context:** David is a graduate student who spends most of his day attending classes, studying, and completing assignments in the engineering buildings. He often has limited time to eat between afternoon and evening classes. He usually eats alone and prefers affordable Chinese restaurants close to campus where he can get a quick meal.

### What Do They Want to Do with the Tool?

* **Primary Tasks:**
  * Find Chinese restaurants within a 10-minute walk from the UW engineering buildings.
  * Find restaurants with meals under $15 per person.
  * Check restaurant menus for quick meals and takeout options.
  * Compare walking distances and food safety inspection records.
* **Operational Workflows:**
  * **Standard Operations:** David opens the Chinese Restaurant Guide and searches for restaurants near the UW engineering buildings. He compares walking distances and selects restaurants within 10 minutes. He opens menu links to find meals under $15 and checks food safety inspection records. He then selects a nearby restaurant and uses the map to find directions.
  * **Edge/Exception Operations:** If no restaurants are available within a 10-minute walk, David increases the distance to 15 minutes. If a restaurant does not have a menu link, he checks another restaurant. If the restaurant is closed or has no takeout options, he selects another nearby restaurant that fits his schedule.

### What Needs and Desires Do They Have for the Tool?

* **Functional Needs:** The tool must provide restaurant locations, walking distances, food safety inspection records, and menu links. It should allow David to compare nearby restaurants and easily access their websites for additional information.
* **Desires / Experience Preferences:** David wants a fast and simple search process because he has limited time between classes. He prefers a clear list of nearby restaurants with walking distances and easy access to menus. He does not want to spend much time searching through different websites.

### What is Their Skill Level?

* **Technical Proficiency:** High. David is comfortable using computers, websites, online maps, and basic programming tools. However, he has never used this restaurant guide before.
* **Domain Knowledge:** David is familiar with the UW campus and nearby streets. He knows how to compare restaurant prices and walking distances, but he has limited knowledge of official restaurant food safety inspection records.

# Use Case: Discovering Asian Cultural Destinations in Seattle

### Who is the User?

* **Persona/Role:** Yuki Tanaka, a first-year international undergraduate student at UW.
* **Context:** Yuki recently moved to Seattle and is not familiar with local Asian restaurants, cafés, grocery stores, or cultural activities. She wants to explore places connected to Asian cultures during her free time.

### What Do They Want to Do with the Tool?

* **Primary Tasks:**
  * Browse Asian restaurants, cafés, grocery stores, and cultural activities by category.
  * Search for destinations by name or keyword.
  * View destination descriptions, addresses, price ranges, and available reviews.
  * Explore destination locations on an interactive map and open map links for directions.
* **Operational Workflows:**
  * **Standard Operations:** Yuki opens SeattleAsianLife, selects a category or enters a search keyword, and browses matching destinations in a list or map view. She opens a destination to review its description, address, price range, and available reviews, then follows its map link for directions.
  * **Edge/Exception Operations:** If no destinations match her search, Yuki reads the no-results message and adjusts her keyword or category. If destination details are missing, she checks which information is unavailable before choosing another listing. If the map does not load, she uses the displayed address or external map link to locate the destination.

### What Needs and Desires Do They Have for the Tool?

* **Functional Needs:** Category browsing, keyword search, destination details, an interactive map, and external map links. The system must clearly indicate when no results are found or destination information is unavailable.
* **Desires / Experience Preferences:** Clear categories, an intuitive interface, and reliable descriptions and location information. Yuki wants to discover places without knowing their names in advance and receive simple guidance when she needs to adjust her search.

### What is Their Skill Level?

* **Technical Proficiency:** Medium. Yuki uses smartphones, websites, and Google Maps regularly but has never used a local destination guide.
* **Domain Knowledge:** Limited knowledge of Seattle's neighborhoods and Asian cultural destinations. She can read basic listings and follow map directions but needs descriptions and categories to identify places to explore.

# Use Case: Comparing Affordable Destinations for a Weekend Outing

### Who is the User?

* **Persona/Role:** Ethan Lin, an undergraduate student at UW who has a limited budget for weekend outings.
* **Context:** Ethan wants to explore Seattle with friends but has limited time and money. He needs to compare several destinations before choosing an option that meets his preferences, travel time, and budget.

### What Do They Want to Do with the Tool?

* **Primary Tasks:**
  * Filter destinations by category, location, distance, and price range.
  * Sort results and compare prices, estimated costs, locations, and available ratings.
  * Check whether price information or other details are unavailable or outdated.
  * Choose a destination that meets his preferences, travel time, and budget.
* **Operational Workflows:**
  * **Standard Operations:** Ethan opens SeattleAsianLife, searches for destinations or selects a category, and applies price range, category, and distance filters. He reviews and sorts the updated results, opens several destinations to compare their prices, estimated costs, locations, and available ratings, then selects an option within his budget and checks its location on the map.
  * **Edge/Exception Operations:** If the filters produce no results, Ethan relaxes or resets them and searches again. If prices or ratings are missing or outdated, he reads the availability labels and update dates when provided, checks a linked source when available, or chooses another listing. If location access is unavailable, he selects a location manually to continue comparing destinations.

### What Needs and Desires Do They Have for the Tool?

* **Functional Needs:** Filters for category, location, distance, and price range; sorting controls; consistently displayed comparison information; and clear labels for estimated, missing, or outdated prices and other details. The system must allow him to adjust or reset filters when no results are found.
* **Desires / Experience Preferences:** Simple filtering and sorting, clearly displayed estimated costs, and consistent destination details that make comparisons easy. Ethan wants to understand the limits of the available information before deciding where to spend his time and money.

### What is Their Skill Level?

* **Technical Proficiency:** Medium. Ethan is comfortable using search engines, basic filters, and online maps but has never used a specialized destination recommendation tool.
* **Domain Knowledge:** Familiar with planning a student budget and comparing prices, but has limited knowledge of Seattle's destinations. He needs help evaluating local options and distinguishing estimated costs from current prices.
